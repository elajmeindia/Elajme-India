Your treatment journey in India: a patient guide

A simple overview of how an international treatment enquiry can move from initial information to hospital review and practical travel coordination.

Start your enquiry
1. Tell us about the medical need

Share a brief description of the condition, diagnosis if known, patient age and country, and the treatment being considered if already advised.

2. Organise relevant medical information

Depending on the case, a hospital may request consultation notes, investigations, imaging or pathology reports, previous treatment records and current medications.

3. Hospital or specialist review

Relevant information can be coordinated with an appropriate hospital or specialist team. The medical team determines the clinical opinion, recommended next steps and whether further information is required.

4. Review available treatment and cost information

Where provided by the hospital, review the proposed approach and available estimate carefully. Final treatment and costs may change after in-person assessment or additional investigations.

5. Plan the treatment visit

After deciding to proceed, practical coordination may include appointments, hospital admission planning, travel-related documentation, accommodation or local logistics as applicable.

6. Treatment and follow-up

Clinical care remains under the treating hospital and medical team. Practical communication and coordination can continue through the treatment journey and, where appropriate, after return home.

Preparing information for a hospital review

Organising the available records can make the initial case review easier. Patients can start with a short summary of the medical problem, the diagnosis if known, recent relevant reports, previous treatment information and a current medicine list.

Relevant records may include laboratory results, imaging reports, pathology reports, discharge summaries and notes from major previous procedures. The exact information needed depends on the individual case, and the hospital or specialist may request additional documents after the first review.

You do not need to delay an initial enquiry because every record is not available. Start with the information you have and keep the documents clearly labelled where possible.

Before travelling for treatment in India

Once a hospital or specialist has reviewed the case and the patient decides to proceed, confirm the planned appointment or admission dates before finalising travel. Keep copies of the hospital communication, medical records, passport and relevant travel documents together so they are easy to access during the journey.

If the treating team has given instructions about medicines, fasting, investigations or arrival time, follow the hospital's directions. Patients who may need assistance during travel or recovery should also consider who will accompany them and discuss practical needs with the hospital in advance.

Remote consultation and second-opinion coordination

Some cases may be suitable for an initial remote review or consultation before travel, depending on the hospital, specialist and available medical information. Elajme India can help coordinate the enquiry and share available records with the selected medical team. The hospital or doctor decides whether a remote consultation is appropriate and what additional information is required.
