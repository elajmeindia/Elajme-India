After Hospital Discharge in India: A Guide for International Patients

Leaving the hospital is an important step, but recovery and follow-up continue afterwards. International patients should understand their discharge instructions, keep medical records, and confirm when it is safe to travel home.

Understand your discharge summary

Before leaving, ask for a written discharge summary covering the diagnosis, treatment received, test results, pending reports, and the name of the treating team. Confirm whom to contact if questions arise after discharge.

Review every medicine

Ask your clinician or pharmacist to explain each medicine, its dose, timing, duration and possible side effects. Check whether you should continue medicines you were taking before admission. Keep prescriptions and medicines in their original packaging when travelling.

Plan recovery and daily care

Follow the specific advice given for activity, food, wound care, bathing and rest. Do not assume that all patients can resume normal activity at the same pace. If you need dressing changes, physiotherapy or help at home, arrange them before discharge.

Know when to seek urgent help

Ask your medical team which warning signs apply to your condition. Severe breathing difficulty, chest pain, uncontrolled bleeding, new confusion or sudden weakness require urgent medical assessment. For other concerning symptoms, contact the treating team promptly.

Confirm follow-up appointments

Before departure, confirm the next appointment, whether it can be conducted remotely, and which reports or photographs may be needed. Ask who will review pending test results and how updates will reach your doctor at home.

Keep copies of your medical records

Request copies of your discharge summary, prescriptions, investigation reports, imaging and procedure notes where available. Store secure digital copies and keep important documents accessible during travel. Protect personal medical information when sharing it.

Ask about fitness to fly

Do not book your return flight solely on the basis of a discharge date. Ask the treating clinician when flying is medically appropriate, especially after surgery or serious illness. Check airline requirements for medical clearance, mobility support, oxygen or medications when relevant.

Coordinate the journey home

Confirm transport, accommodation if additional recovery time is needed, and a reliable contact for hospital communication. Share your care plan with a trusted companion and arrange follow-up with a clinician in your home country.
