Foot and ankle care coordination

Podiatry and foot care in India for international patients

Elajme India helps international patients coordinate specialist review for foot and ankle concerns in India. The treating team may assess pain, walking difficulties, wounds or deformity and consider diabetes or circulation problems before recommending an appropriate care pathway.

Care depends on the foot or ankle condition

Foot and ankle assessment

The treating team may review pain, swelling, deformity, wounds, walking difficulty, previous injuries or surgery, footwear concerns and the effect of the condition on daily activity.

Medical factors

Diabetes, circulation problems, nerve symptoms, infection risk and other medical conditions can affect foot care and may require review with additional specialties.

Treatment and rehabilitation

Management may involve conservative care, footwear or orthotic advice, wound care, rehabilitation or surgery depending on the diagnosis and specialist assessment.

Elajme India provides international patient coordination and general information. We do not diagnose conditions, prescribe treatment or guarantee treatment acceptance or outcomes.

What to share for an initial review

Useful records may include a description of the foot or ankle problem, photographs when relevant, recent specialist notes, X-rays or other imaging, diabetes and circulation records, wound reports, previous surgery details and current medicines.

1. Describe the problem

Share where the problem is located, when it started, pain or swelling, walking limitations, any wound or skin change, and previous treatment.

2. Send imaging and clinical records

Provide available X-rays, ultrasound, CT or MRI reports, orthopaedic or foot-care notes, wound records and details of previous procedures or rehabilitation.

3. Include relevant medical history

If applicable, include diabetes control, vascular or nerve assessments, infection history and current medicines. The treating team determines which further tests or specialties are appropriate.
